A wearable that eases spasticity in children with cerebral palsy

Tonicity is a soft sleeve that reads a child's muscle signals and gently stimulates the opposing muscle, helping the tight one relax. It adapts in real time, pauses itself if a spasm starts, and targets a price of ₹8,000–9,000 so families can use it every day at home.

We have built a prototype and companion app and received ethics clearance for testing. We are now seeking seed funding for safety testing and a first pilot with children.

Spasticity needs daily care. Most families can't get it.

Cerebral palsy affects 2 to 4 of every 1,000 children born, and about 80% have the spastic form: muscles stay tight, pull joints out of line and make walking harder. Left unmanaged through the growth years, it leads to contractures, deformities and surgery.

Botulinum toxin injections

They work, but need a specialist every 3–6 months and are painful and costly for families.

Oral medicines such as baclofen

They act on the whole body and can cause drowsiness and weakness.

Physiotherapy

It means frequent trips to a clinic, and gains fade without daily practice at home.

Imported stimulation suits

They can be used at home but cost around ₹6 lakh, out of reach for most families in India.

How Tonicity works

Tonicity uses reciprocal inhibition, the body's own reflex: when one muscle contracts, its opposing muscle is signalled to relax.

  1. Sense

    EMG sensors in the sleeve read tone in the tight muscle; motion sensors track the limb.

  2. Decide

    An on-device algorithm starts at a low intensity and adjusts each cycle from the live muscle signal.

  3. Stimulate

    Gentle electrical pulses contract the opposing muscle, helping the spastic one release.

  4. Stop safely

    Each cycle ends after 30 contractions or at signs of fatigue, and stops instantly on a sudden jerk. Once the day's target is met, the device locks for 12 hours.

Built for growing children

Sleeves, socks and gloves in child sizes. Electrode and sensor modules swap out as the child grows, instead of replacing the whole device.

Comfortable to wear

Mesh-backed hydrogel electrodes placed over each muscle's motor point, held steady by a semi-rigid frame.

Connected to care

A companion app shows parents each session. If an electrode comes loose or a part fails, stimulation stops and the parent and physiotherapist are alerted.

Who it's for

Children with spastic cerebral palsy at GMFCS levels I to III, who walk with or without aids, from toddlers starting weight-bearing therapy onward. It is designed for focal spasticity in the legs and arms, and our first study includes children under 13.

Care starts in the clinic: a clinician selects and fits the child and trains the family. Home sessions then run within the settings the clinic has set, with regular re-fitting as the child grows.

What makes it different

Most home stimulation devices run a programme set in advance. Tonicity listens to the muscle and adapts every cycle, is made for home use, and is designed around a growing child.

Our first clinical study

Our feasibility study at Kasturba Medical College, Mangalore has ethics committee clearance, and CTRI registration is under way. It will test whether children can use Tonicity safely and regularly, and measure changes in movement, muscle tone and walking.

Design
Pre–post feasibility study over 12–18 months
Participants
20 children with cerebral palsy, under 13, at GMFCS levels I–III
Setting
Physiotherapy departments of hospitals affiliated to KMC Mangalore, with device engineering at NITK Surathkal
What we measure
Gross motor function (GMFM-88), spasticity (Modified Ashworth Scale), muscle strength, walking speed and video gait analysis, and hours of use logged by the device

Why stimulation: in a 2022 scoping review of 26 studies with 558 participants with spastic CP, NMES reduced spasticity in most studies (Greve et al., Frontiers in Physiology).
